Mapping Urban Happiness: A Critical Look at the Happy City Index

What makes a city happy?

Overview

Urban happiness is increasingly recognized as a critical indicator of quality of life, influencing everything from resident well-being and productivity to economic growth and social cohesion. As cities continue to expand globally, understanding what makes a city happy becomes essential for urban planners, policymakers, and residents alike. This project investigates the question: What do happy cities have in common with each other, and what makes a city happy?

Using data from the Happy City Index, which ranks cities based on six key indicators—Citizens, Governance, Environment, Economy, Health, and Mobility—this analysis explores patterns and commonalities among the world's happiest urban centers.

The methodology involved webscraping city data from the Happy City Index, cleaning and processing the dataset using Python, and creating interactive visualizations with Folium (for geospatial mapping) and Plotly (for comparative analysis). Through these visualizations and statistical analyses, the project identifies trends in indicator scores and examines how different dimensions of urban life contribute to overall happiness.

This analysis has notable limitations: the Happy City Index only considers the top 200 cities globally, and as evident from the geographic distribution, large regions including much of Africa, Asia, and South America are underrepresented or entirely absent from the dataset. These gaps highlight the need for more inclusive global urban happiness assessments that capture diverse geographic and economic contexts.

Geographic Analysis

This interactive map visualizes the geographic distribution of the world's 200 happiest cities according to the Happy City Index. Each circle represents a city, with darker colors indicating happier cities and lighter colors showing lower rankings. Larger circles represent cities with higher total happiness scores. Click on any marker to view detailed information about that city's rank and indicator scores.

Indicator Trends

This scatterplot displays the six indicator scores for all 200 cities, arranged from highest to lowest ranked. Citizen scores show the most consistent linear decline from left to right, though the indicator's meaning remains somewhat ambiguous in measuring civic satisfaction and engagement. Mobility scores are notably scattered with high variability across cities, suggesting that transportation and accessibility systems vary widely even among similarly-ranked cities.

City Comparison

This interactive radar chart compares the six indicator scores across selected cities, with each colored shape representing a different city's performance profile. Use the slider to adjust how many top-ranked and bottom-ranked cities are displayed simultaneously—higher values on each axis indicate better performance in that specific indicator.
